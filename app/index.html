Module 1: The Human Factor in Cybersecurity
Why attackers target psychological heuristics rather than technical vulnerabilities.

Over 80% of enterprise breaches originate with social engineering. While modern network infrastructure deploys hardened intrusion prevention systems, the human mind remains susceptible to emotional manipulation:

  • Manufactured Urgency: Imposing tight timeframes (e.g. 4 hours) to trigger cognitive panic.
  • Authority Pretexts: Impersonating the IT Helpdesk, Dean, or C-Level executives to force blind compliance.
  • Curiosity & Logistics: Fabricating tracking notifications (#FX-98214-DEL) to exploit the desire for expected goods.
Module 2: Anatomy of Email Deception & Header Analysis
Deconstructing the 5 universal indicators of a phishing email.

Before clicking any link or opening attachments, perform the 5-Point Header Verification:

1. Sender Address Verification:

Display names are arbitrary. Always inspect the domain after the @ symbol. Look for typosquatting (e.g., micros0ft.com).

2. Hyperlink Destination:

Hover without clicking. Ensure the destination protocol and root domain precisely match the official service.

Module 3: Quishing (QR Phishing) & Adversary-in-the-Middle
Exploring modern evasion vectors that bypass Secure Email Gateways.

Quishing (QR Code Phishing): Threat actors embed target URLs within QR images. This bypasses static email filters and forces targets onto unmanaged smartphones lacking enterprise antivirus protections.

Golden Rule: Never scan a QR code received via email to perform password updates, banking, or corporate benefits election.

Executive Abstract & Overview

This research project designed, deployed, and empirically evaluated a controlled, ethical phishing simulation and security awareness training pipeline within an isolated laboratory testbed. Utilizing GoPhish, MailHog, and custom web interception services, a closed cohort of 25 consenting participants was subjected to an initial baseline simulation (Campaign 1: IT Credential Expiry). The baseline results revealed critical user susceptibility: a 64.0% link click rate and a 48.0% credential submission rate, with only 8.0% of participants reporting the threat.

Following the baseline test, an interactive Security Awareness Training program was administered, covering anatomical red-flag identification, credential hygiene, and quishing defenses, concluded by a graded 10-question evaluation (cohort mean score: 88.0%). A subsequent post-training simulation (Campaign 2: Courier Exception & QR-code Quishing) demonstrated remarkable behavioral improvement: the click rate plummeted to 12.0% (an 81.25% reduction in susceptibility), credential submissions collapsed to 4.0% (a 91.67% reduction), and active security reporting surged to 76.0% (an 850% increase).
